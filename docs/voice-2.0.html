churpie. · voice 2.0 · November 2026

Write like a postcard from someone who loves them.

Builds on the April voice guide; nothing in it is retired. Version 2.0 adds a literary anchor, a fixed vocabulary, a register for each screen, and the rules that came out of rewriting every line in the live app.

The anchor

“Hope” is the thing with feathers – / That perches in the soul – / And sings the tune without the words – / And never stops – at all –Emily Dickinson, 1891 · public domain

Churpie is a small bird's sound. Each message is a small chirp; together they make a song for one person. Dickinson is the model for how we write: short, plain words, said with total conviction, no decoration, and hope that is quiet rather than loud. The second model is the postcard: very little space, so every word earns its place, and it's always signed by a person.

The line appears once in the product (the home page footer). We quote it, we don't imitate it. Our sentences stay modern and plain.

Four tests for every line

  • Would a person say it out loud to a friend? If it sounds like software (“submit”, “stitch”, “clip ids”, “watch link”), rewrite it.
  • Does it name a person? Use “Rosa” and “Maria” wherever we know them. Never “the host”, “the recipient” or “contributors”.
  • Is it true? Never promise what the app doesn't do. (We removed “a backup copy of your links”; no email is ever sent.)
  • Does it permit, not instruct? “When you're ready” beats “Press the button”. One gentle ask beats three rules.

Mechanics

  • Case: lowercase only for the wordmark, the home page's serif headlines and the hand-drawn card art. Everything else is sentence case, including link previews and page titles.
  • No exclamation marks. In shared texts, the playful version is a whisper: “(Shh, don't tell Rosa.)”
  • No emoji or symbol shorthand in prose (“~”, “→” as a verb). Icons always carry a word.
  • Refer to buttons by their words, never their color (“Tap Keep it forever”, not “the pink button”).
  • Plurals are real: “1 person”, never “1 people”.
  • Recipient-facing surfaces never sell. The end card says “12 people showed up for you.” with only churpie.me beneath it; no “make one” prompt on someone's gift.

Experience rules (added after the second review)

  • The main action is on the first screen. A guest sees the ask, the tips and Record without scrolling. The camera box stays small until the camera is on.
  • One loud button per moment. On the share screen that's sharing, not the board. On the board, "Make the reel" only turns pink once 3 or more people are in.
  • No progress toward a goal that doesn't exist. Show who's in, by name, not a bar that reads as "not enough".
  • Fold what's optional. House rules and longer messages live under "More options". Tips start folded, with thumbnails so they still invite a tap.
  • Anything that looks tappable does something. The moment chips on the home page start that kind of card.
  • Self-sufficient, always. The product never tells anyone to contact a person: not the host, not us, nobody. Every limit (a full card, a closed card, a missed deadline) explains itself in one sentence and offers a next step the reader can take alone, such as "Start a card for Rosa". No personal names, emails or support addresses appear anywhere in the product.
  • Headlines break in balance. No word left alone on the last line.

Where the copy lives

Pages: public/*.html and public/app.js. Server messages and link previews: src/index.js. Moments, questions and tones: src/catalog.js. Card art and reel text: stitcher/cards/card.html, stitcher/render.js, stitcher/stitch.js. Machine-only errors (Unauthorized, Bad key) stay technical on purpose; no person ever sees them.

When in doubt, read it aloud as if you were handing someone a postcard.
